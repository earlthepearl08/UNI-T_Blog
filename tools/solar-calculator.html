DC Cable & Fusing

The two-stage 125% method and the larger-of ampacity test, per NEC 690.8 and 690.9(B).

This tool will not give you an ampacity. It asks you for one. No Philippine mm² ampacity value could be verified against the printed PEC, and PV cable to EN 50618 / IEC 62930 is rated on a 60 °C ambient basis, not the 30 °C basis of building wire. Take the figure from your cable's own datasheet.
AC circuits take one 125% factor, never the irradiance factor.

Commissioning Tests

The IEC 62446-1 Category 1 sequence in order, with the insulation test voltage computed for your array and a check that your instrument is actually rated for it.

A PV array cannot be switched off. It is live whenever light reaches it. A DC arc has no current zero and therefore no guaranteed extinction point — MC4-type connectors, touch-safe fuse holders and many tool-operated isolators are non-load-break devices and must never be opened under load.
The most dangerous instrument error in PV work: an AC-only (current-transformer) clamp reads 0 A on a live DC string. A technician who uses one to "verify zero current" before pulling an MC4 will read zero and pull a fully loaded string into an arc. Only a Hall-effect AC/DC clamp reads DC. Clamp one conductor only — clamping both poles also reads zero. And a clamp measures current; it does not prove a circuit is dead. Check voltage separately with a rated meter.
